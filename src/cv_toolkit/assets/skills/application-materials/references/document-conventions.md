# Resume and cover-letter conventions

General conventions for application documents, adapted from the `resume-cover-letter` skill in [jezweb/claude-skills](https://github.com/jezweb/claude-skills) (MIT licence, notice below). Apply them while drafting and check them during review. They rank below three things: actual evidence (never trade a supported fact for a convention), employer instructions, and the owner's recorded writing preferences in `writing-voice.md`. When a recorded preference conflicts with a convention here, for example a prescribed opening sentence, follow the preference and do not report the convention as a defect.

## Region

Work out the region from the job location and the employer, and ask only when it is unclear and changes the document.

| Element | AU/NZ | US | UK |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Usual name | resume or CV | resume | CV |
| Usual length | two pages, three for long careers | one page under about ten years of experience, two at most | two pages |
| Photo, date of birth, age | leave out | leave out | leave out |
| Work rights or visa | state when relevant | leave out unless asked | state when relevant |
| Address | city and state, never the street | city and state | city |
| Referees | leave out; list two only when asked | leave out | leave out |

The effective policy's page limit still decides the length; the table only informs the choice when the employer sets none. Sign-offs: Kind regards in AU/NZ, Sincerely or Kind regards in the US and UK.

## Section order

Choose the order that shows the candidate's strongest evidence first, then keep it consistent.

- Early career (up to about three years): contact, summary, education, experience (internships, part-time, volunteering), skills, projects and certifications.
- Mid-career (about three to ten years): contact, summary, experience, skills, education, certifications.
- Senior (ten years or more): contact, summary, an optional key-achievements section, experience, board or advisory roles, education, memberships.

List several roles at one employer under the employer once, each role as its own dated entry, following the chronology rules in the drafting workflow.

## Bullets: challenge, action, result

Build each bullet from what the challenge or context was, what the candidate did and what changed as a result. Every bullet should answer "so what?". Supported results can be a percentage, an amount, a scale (users, team size, tests per release), a time saving, a ranking, or a qualitative outcome the evidence states (adopted by two teams, unblocked a release). Prefer bullets with a supported result, but never estimate a number the evidence does not hold and never write a vague result such as "significantly reduced" or "consistently exceeded targets". An honest action with no result is acceptable. Asking the owner for a missing result follows the evidence-strengthening rules, not a quota.

Open with a verb that matches the contribution:

| Contribution | Verbs |
| --- | --- |
| Leadership | led, directed, managed, mentored, established |
| Creation | developed, designed, built, launched, implemented, introduced |
| Improvement | increased, improved, streamlined, optimised, reduced |
| Analysis | analysed, evaluated, assessed, identified, investigated |
| Communication | presented, negotiated, facilitated, coordinated, advised, authored |
| Technical | engineered, automated, configured, deployed, integrated, migrated |

Never open with "responsible for", "helped with", "assisted in", "involved in" or "participated in": they state proximity, not contribution. A verb must not claim more ownership than the evidence gives, so "led" or "deployed" need support, and joint work keeps verbs such as co-designed or collaborated.

## What to leave out

- "References available on request", objective statements, salary expectations and reasons for leaving.
- Roles older than about fifteen years unless they are relevant; secondary-school education once there is a degree.
- Hobbies, unless they matter to the role or the owner asks for them.

## Summary

A summary positions the candidate; it is not a personality description. A useful shape is the candidate's role identity, years and domain, one signature supported achievement, and, only when the owner wants it, what they are looking for next. Avoid "motivated professional with strong communication skills" phrasing.

## Cover letter

Keep it under one page, with the same header as the resume, addressed to a named person when the JD or a checked source names one ("Dear Hiring Manager" otherwise).

Match the tone to the employer, judged from the frozen JD and checked sources:

| Employer | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Startup or product company | direct and conversational |
| Corporate or enterprise | professional and measured |
| Government or public sector | formal, addressing the selection criteria |
| Creative agency | personality forward |
| Non-profit | mission-aligned, through the owner's actual reasons |

Common mistakes to avoid or report:

- Rehashing the resume: the letter adds context and argument, it does not repeat bullets.
- A generic opening that would fit any job, unless it is the owner's recorded opening preference.
- No reference to this employer or role: if the letter could go to fifty companies, it is too generic. Company facts still come only from the frozen JD or checked sources, and motivation only from the owner's answers.
- Overselling ("the perfect candidate") or underselling ("I know I don't have much experience, but").
- Burying the lead: a direct connection to the role (using the employer's product, deep domain experience) belongs near the start, when it is supported.

## Special circumstances

- Career change: lead with transferable, supported skills rather than titles, and let the summary bridge the two fields.
- Employment gap: never change or blur dates. Activity during the gap (courses, freelance work, projects) can go on the resume when supported. Whether the letter mentions the gap, and how, is the owner's decision; ask them, and follow any recorded preference to leave negatives out.
- Overqualified: the letter answers why this role, using the owner's actual motivation.
- Underqualified: show adjacent supported experience and how similar gaps were closed before. Do not invent readiness, and follow the owner's preference on whether to name the stretch.
