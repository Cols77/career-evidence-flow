# Voice and artifact review

This reference owns the shared editorial criteria for drafting and independent resume/letter review. Apply them within those existing steps and their two-round repair limit; do not create another reviewer, skill, approval stage or detector-scoring loop.

## Private writing preferences

Read `writing-voice.md` in the workspace's configured `[paths].profile` directory, if present. This is an optional agent-maintained working note, not a new CLI record or source of candidate facts. Reuse existing canonical preference records and link to them rather than copying their answers. Record only writing preferences, provenance references and paths to authentic user-supplied samples. Keep all samples and preferences in the explicit private workspace, never in installed skills or public fixtures.

Current user instructions and employer requirements take precedence. Update the note when the user states or corrects a preference, recording the source once through the existing experience-capture process when canonical capture is needed. Distinguish confirmed preferences from suggested defaults. Do not infer a preferred professional voice from casual chat, mistakes, nationality or generated drafts. Without confirmed preferences or a sample, use direct, specific, professional language as a default and proceed; a missing sample is not a blocker. Ask only when the answer materially affects the application.

Include the note, any samples actually used and their referenced preferences in the existing review packet and selected-file checkpoint. A style-input change invalidates the affected voice review through the existing freshness procedure.

## Editorial pass and factual recheck

During drafting, edit once for the intended reader: use concrete supported actions, remove filler and stock enthusiasm, and repair repetitive openings or sentence structures where that improves readability. Match confirmed vocabulary and voice. Retain useful engineering terms, accurate JD keywords, normal resume bullets/headings, deliberate repetition and meaningful punctuation. Do not enforce word blacklists, punctuation bans or artificial sentence-length targets.

For resume bullets, always start with an action verb that matches the person's contribution, such as developed, evaluated, coordinated or contributed. Do not lead with a role label, tool name, date, or "responsible for". Preserve joint ownership with verbs such as collaborated or co-designed; an action-led opening must not inflate responsibility. Present factual inventories such as skills, qualifications and languages as labelled paragraphs or rows rather than inventing actions to turn them into bullets.

Select skills for meaning as well as factual support. Each entry should name a useful capability, technical method or relevant tool that a reader can recognise and connect to evidence. Routine workflow artifacts, dataset names and minor implementation steps usually belong in the experience that explains their use. For example, use supported experiment tracking or model evaluation capabilities and the relevant tools; do not promote configuration files or saved checkpoints into standalone skills simply because they appear in the evidence. Dataset preparation can be a capability; a particular dataset usually supplies context for its accomplishment. This is a contextual editorial judgment, not a keyword ban: retain a specific artifact or dataset when the role explicitly requires expertise in it and the evidence establishes that expertise. Do not upgrade routine use into reproducibility engineering, MLOps, deployment or lifecycle ownership without support.

Review each bullet for one clear contribution. Split independently meaningful deliverables, responsibilities or work streams into separate action-led bullets, even when they share an employer or tool. Keep an action's methods, constraints and measured result together: multiple verbs, sentences, tools or list items alone are not a reason to split. For example, a fictional bullet combining development of a sensor driver with design of an independent testbench should become two bullets, while parser validation using boundary cases and its measured result can remain one. If space is tight, shorten or prioritise supported content instead of recombining unrelated contributions. Check every resulting bullet against the evidence after splitting.

Compare the rewrite with the source and support notes before rendering. Preserve numbers, approximate values, dates, uncertainty, negative results, team attribution and prototype/production distinctions. Do not invent opinions, motivation, anecdotes or reactions to add personality. Never introduce errors or weaken clear writing to influence a detector. Repairs use the existing factual review and rerendering process; they do not create a second factual checklist or bypass employer AI-use rules.

Independent reviewers assess the final text against these criteria and the private writing preferences. Explicitly check action-led openings and whether bullets combine independent contributions; report the exact opening or combined passage and a narrow repair, or no findings. A vocabulary pattern is an editorial observation, not proof of AI authorship. No authorship probability or promise of undetectability is warranted. Do not upload candidate documents to detector or rewriting services as part of this workflow.

In the existing alignment/content verdict, explicitly assess skills selection and granularity separately from factual support. Identify any entries to remove, consolidate or move into experience, with the specific reason; report no findings only after making that judgment. A factual pass does not imply a useful skills section. A focused revision may reuse a recorded review of unchanged content, but must say which content was newly reviewed and which earlier findings it relies on; never present a narrow pass as a new whole-document semantic review.

## Cover-letter fluency pass

After selecting supported content and before rendering, read the whole letter in sequence for transitions, sentence rhythm and natural reading. Assess how it would sound spoken without claiming an audio check unless one actually occurred. A sequence of accurate sentences is not enough: the reader should understand why each sentence and paragraph follows the previous one.

Review the introduction explicitly before accepting the draft. Orient the reader to the application and give enough supported context about the candidate or opportunity for the interest to follow naturally. Check the first sentence, the first two or three sentences together, and the handoff into the first example. A declaration of attraction or ambition can feel abrupt when it arrives before that context; a subject line alone does not establish a welcoming prose introduction. Prefer a brief purposeful lead-in over either an unexplained enthusiasm statement or a long ceremonial preamble. Respect the candidate's actual opening preferences. Do not impose a universal phrase, sentence order or blacklist: revise the passage that feels abrupt, not every occurrence of a word. No invented familiarity, personal history or generic flattery may be added to soften the opening.

Where the text moves from motivation to qualifications, make the substantive connection visible: how the proposed work lets this candidate contribute to the stated need or purpose. Use the actual role and supported experience; do not invent a personal anecdote, past learning, causal history or achieved impact to supply that bridge. Generic fit labels such as a role being a relevant application of experience can leave the connection unexplained. Adding a transition word alone does not repair a missing idea.

Check adjacent sentence openings and clause patterns, abrupt changes of subject, overloaded sentences, repetitive summaries and abstract language that makes the reader pause. Reorder, combine or split where it helps; preserve technical precision, useful detail and necessary qualifications. Let sentence length and rhythm follow the thought rather than an imposed variation quota. Do not add filler, forced warmth, stock connectors or colloquial wording merely to sound human. A clear passage may need no change.

Then read sentence by sentence for literal meaning, not just overall flow. Resolve named organisations, teams, products and projects against the JD or checked sources; examine the verbs, prepositions and pronouns that connect them. A correctly sourced name can still be used misleadingly. For a fictional product called FieldLens, "At FieldLens, I would join your engineers" implies an employer; "Working on FieldLens with your engineers" describes product work. "At" remains appropriate for an actual employer or location: this is a meaning check, not a phrase blacklist. If a relationship is unknown, use supported neutral wording or flag the ambiguity rather than inventing a corporate structure. Read the repaired sentence with its neighbours and recheck factual scope.

Review the conclusion explicitly: read the last substantive paragraph, closing paragraph and sign-off together. The closing should follow naturally from the contribution just described and give the reader a courteous sense of completion. A brief invitation to discuss the relevant contribution and, where appropriate, thanks for considering the application can do this; neither is a required formula. Flag an abrupt switch to location/logistics, a bare sign-off, a repeated mini-resume or a presumptive demand for an interview when it disrupts the ending. Keep the closing proportionate and the sign-off appropriate to the letter's tone. Do not add unsupported availability, relocation, work rights, enthusiasm or outcomes to make it sound warmer. A short closing can be complete; no fixed sentence count, mandatory thank-you or universal phrase is required. Recheck the transition after editing and inspect the rendered closing/signature for crowding or page spill.

Record substantive fluency repairs, or a brief reason the existing flow works, in the existing drafting notes. Then recheck the edited claims against the evidence. Independent letter reviewers must assess these same whole-letter and adjacent-passage connections within the existing voice/comprehension verdict, citing the exact transition or rhythm problem and a narrow repair, or explaining why no useful change is needed. Factual and persuasiveness verdicts do not substitute for this fluency assessment. Keep the existing reviewer handoff and two-round limit; no additional reviewer, interview, detector score or automatic hook gate is introduced.

A voice/comprehension no-findings verdict requires an explicit introduction observation: quote or identify the opening and explain how it establishes context and leads into the candidate's reason for applying and first example. If it starts abruptly or conflicts with a supplied preference, record a concrete finding and repair it within the existing review cycle; do not waive it because later paragraphs flow or the claims are supported. This is a required host drafting/review criterion, not a mechanically enforced language-quality gate.

Also record a brief sentence-level meaning observation identifying the named-entity relationships checked (or their absence), with the affected wording and repair or why it is appropriate. A paragraph-transition explanation, sourced company fact, passed artifact check or earlier no-findings report does not supply that observation. When the user identifies a missed wording defect, reopen the affected editorial verdict even if the document bytes have not changed; carry forward only unaffected findings and disclose their scope.

A voice/comprehension no-findings verdict also requires a closing observation identifying the final body-to-closing transition, invitation or other appropriate ending, and sign-off. Explain why this sequence feels complete, or cite the abrupt passage and a narrow repair. A strong introduction, supported body or passing page check cannot substitute for evaluating the ending. Record this within the existing review rather than adding a separate approval stage.

## Final artifact observations

Run the existing `cv check` on every exact rendered output. Its `artifact_audit` accompanies the ordinary identity, extraction and policy checks for both document kinds. It reports Unicode format-character code points/counts; for readable PDFs, standard metadata field names, counts of other metadata fields and attachments, XMP presence, watermark annotations and page-content non-painting text-mode operators. Metadata values, attachment names and extracted text are omitted from the JSON report.

`observed` means these checks ran. `unavailable` leaves the audit unverified and requires disclosure or repair. An indicator does not automatically fail document checks: renderers commonly write Creator/Producer metadata, and Unicode format characters can be legitimate. Review flagged observations in context. Do not strip metadata, signatures, genuine provenance or characters automatically; do not claim these observations establish authorship or the absence of all watermarks.

The PDF operator check covers page-stream text modes 3 and 7 only. It does not establish actual visibility or inspect form streams, opacity, clipping, white/off-page text or image watermarks. Use the existing independent visual review on every rendered page and compare extracted text with the source; disclose any unavailable inspection. Subsequent edits or metadata changes require renewed checks and reviews on the resulting bytes before export.
